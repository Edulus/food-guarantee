A Food Guarantee for Canada

The Three Layer Coordinated Price Guarantee

Edward Yersh, BA, McGill University · November 2026

Download the PDFRead the text with notes

Summary

About 9.8 million people in Canada live in households that struggle to afford food. Food insecurity has many causes, and the price of basic food is one that public policy can address directly.

This paper proposes guaranteeing every household access to a basic version of a short list of nutritious staples that Canada produces in abundance, such as flour, oats, beans, lentils, potatoes, carrots, apples, eggs, milk and canola oil. Premium, branded and specialty versions, and everything else in the store, would stay at market prices.

  1. A viable price for producers. Farmers growing these staples would receive a minimum price, set each year by an independent body before planting.
  2. An affordable price in stores. Large grocery chains would sell the basic basket at a published maximum price, with government paying the audited gap between that price and an efficient supply cost.
  3. Enough money to buy food. Households whose incomes remain too low would receive a monthly supplement sized to the regional cost of the basket.

The paper reviews similar policies in Europe, India, Mexico, Malaysia, Hungary and Canada, sets out the ways they have failed, and proposes safeguards against each. Rough estimates put the annual public cost between $3.5 billion and $36 billion, with every assumption stated so readers can test it.
